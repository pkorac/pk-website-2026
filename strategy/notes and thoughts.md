## Case studies: what to include (even with NDAs)

A template that works well for NDA-heavy work:

- Context (industry, scale, constraints – anonymised if needed)
- The problem (why it mattered)
- Your role (leadership, decisions, team shape)
- What you did (process + critical calls)
- What shipped (screens, prototype clips, system diagrams, evaluation plan)
- Impact (even directional metrics: time saved, adoption, revenue risk reduced, delivery speed)
- What you learned / what you’d do next

Aim for 4-6 case studies
A good starting set (based on what I know you’ve been doing):

- An agentic/AI product in a real organisation (DevRev-style work, even anonymised)
- A research / evidence / RAG platform (your evidence portal work is perfect for leadership + systems thinking)
- A small-business AI automation story (Dogsnug – pragmatic, end-to-end, shows delivery)
- One product you’re building (Hot & Cold / RunPear) – shows founder instincts + craft
- One “Lab” piece as a bridge (Tallis Habits is already basically a showcase case study) ￼

## The AI Product Sprint page: make it painfully easy to buy

This should be a dedicated page with:
• A crisp promise: idea → working alpha/MVP in 7 weeks
• Deliverables (what they walk away with)
• Week-by-week shape (lightly, not a novel)
• What makes it different (your angle: design + engineering + AI realities – “AI is more than a chatbot”, which you already say) ￼
• FAQs that handle objections: procurement, NDAs, security, data access, stakeholders, “what if we don’t have data?”, model choices, evaluation, cost control
• A clear CTA: Book a scoping call (and what happens on that call)

Small but powerful detail: include a “Sprint Readiness Checklist” as a downloadable one-pager – it pulls emails and qualifies leads.

## Writing strategy: turn the blog into an engine (not a scrapbook)

Your blog voice is good. The missing piece is editorial alignment: posts should ladder up to “why the sprint exists” and “why Peter is the person to run it”. ￼

I’d organise Writing into 3 tracks:
• AI Product Patterns (agentic UX, human-in-the-loop, evaluation, failure modes, cost/safety guardrails)
• Prototyping That Ships (Nuxt/Capacitor/local-first, toolchains, “how we built the alpha in week 3”)
• Leadership Notes (running sprints, aligning stakeholders, how to make AI work in real orgs)

And then keep running/outdoors as a lighter “Personal” tag if you want – but don’t let it dominate the front page.
